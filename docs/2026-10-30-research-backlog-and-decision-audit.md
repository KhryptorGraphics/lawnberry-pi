# October 30 research status and design-decision audit — Lawnberry Pi

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Review of project research, documentation and decisions through the Pi 5 ↔ Libre Alta Ethernet alternative.  
**Status:** Document/source audit only. No claim that hardware was inspected, energized, software deployed, or manufacturer-specific connector pinouts verified.

## Completed research conclusions **already written into documents**

1. **Two PCA9685 PWM boards** provide 32 *servo signal* channels, enough for two steering servos and the MG995 throttle servo; they do **not** replace ignition, starter, PTO relays, motor power converters, or a stopping mechanism. Recorded in [integrated decisions](2026-10-30-integrated-hardware-and-control-decisions.md) and [owned hardware](2026-10-30-user-confirmed-owned-hardware.md).
2. **Toro model 77603 PTO identification correction:** manufacturer manual identifies the round upper control as **CHOKE** and the rectangular blade-control rocker as **PTO**. Physical connector pins not verified. See [PTO research](2026-10-30-toro-77603-pto-switch-research.md).
3. **Manufacturer part-family identification:** for Toro 77603 serial ≥418961000, catalog lists starter solenoid 117-1197, ignition switch 137-4100, PTO rocker 139-6368 and associated harness; exact Kawasaki starter suffix and actual vehicle connectors are not determined. See [starter/ignition research](2026-10-30-toro-77603-starter-ignition-technical-research.md).
4. **Intercontroller transport choices:** (A) Wi-Fi HaLow peer link and (B) **all Pi 5 ↔ Libre Alta peer communication through direct wired Ethernet** (heartbeats/coordination/fault/status), with HaLow optionally used for off-mower devices. Both are recorded in [networking alternative](2026-10-30-intercontroller-ethernet-alternative.md). Neither has been bench-tested.
5. **Servo throttle mechanical concept:** five printable linkage directions and the requested rightward-offset mounting arrangement are **documented as CAD requirements**, not manufactured or field-tested designs. See [linkage addendum](2026-10-30-throttle-servo-printable-variants-addendum.md).

## Decisions superseding the previous safety-specification documents

The following are the owner's *latest design choices* and are now in [the replacement software-control decision record](2026-10-30-primary-backup-software-control.md). They are **not proof of a fail-safe installation**:

- **Primary/backup roles:** Pi 5 handles normal command authority; Alta maintains independent backup shutdown authority. **If Alta fails, Pi issues blades/PTO OFF and engine OFF; if Pi fails, Alta issues blades/PTO OFF and engine OFF.** The high-level role split is decided; the complete relay/output ownership and takeover test strategy remain open.
- **Watchdogs:** Owner rejected **additional external watchdog modules**. This does not change the fact that a dead or frozen Pi can leave the PCA9685 outputs holding prior PWM, or that software cannot guarantee any stop if both hosts fail. The owner-selected replacement architecture does not include external watchdog modules; that choice does not make stale PWM inherently safe.
- **Emergency stop:** Owner rejected the **added physical E-stop switch**. The added E-stop requirement was removed from the owner-selected *proposed* configuration; this omission cannot establish that the mower is safe for unsupervised use.
- **Single vs simultaneous computer failures:** Owner chose a single-host-failure design goal and regards simultaneous failures as out of scope. The superseded documents called for stopping when *both* hosts were dead; the newer software-only design does not cover both-host or common-cause failures. Mark simultaneous/common-cause failure unprotected under the owner-requested software-only architecture, not "statistically impossible."
- **Peer link:** Both HaLow and Ethernet are documented selectable alternatives; peer heartbeats stay 200 ms, a nominal 3-miss/~600 ms candidate. Real control latency and the mechanical stop delay are unmeasured.

**Interpretation:** These are **owner-selected functional requirements**, not measured stop guarantees. The older prescriptive safety-specification files were deleted at the owner's request. Actual electrical, mechanical, and software takeover behaviors remain to be established with a measured design and appropriate bench testing before deployment.
